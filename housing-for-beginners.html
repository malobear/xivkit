01

Your first steps

Our advice

The order most people follow, start to finish. Tick them off as you go, and this page will remember.

  1. A personal house needs a level 50 combat class and Second Lieutenant rank in a Grand Company. An apartment is the cheap way in while you save up. See Permits and ownership.
  2. A small plot is 3,000,000 to 3,750,000 gil, and the full price is held as a deposit while you wait. Then the Construction Permit is another 450,000 gil, plus furniture.
  3. You get one entry per 9-day cycle (5 days of entry, 4 of results). If you do not win, the deposit is refunded in full (the wiki says within 90 days). If you win and do not finalize, you lose 50% of it.
  4. After you win, buy the size-matched Construction Permit at the Estate Hall. You have 45 days to build or the land is reclaimed. Pick the four required exterior pieces: roof, wall, windows, and door.
  5. Housing Merchants sell the basics for a few thousand gil, and you can preview their items before you buy. See Furnishing cheaply.
  6. A small house holds 300 indoor and 40 yard furnishings. See Limits by size.
  7. Go in at least once every 45 days, or the house is demolished. See Upkeep and demolition.

Once you are in, the beginner guides cover decorating, and sharing your house covers letting friends use it.

Permits and ownership

Myth, busted There is only one housing permit. The "Personal Estate Permit" and "Free Company Estate Permit" do not exist as separate items. After you win a plot, buy the size-matched Construction Permit at the Estate Hall, then build within 45 days.

Personal house

Needs a level 50 combat class and Second Lieutenant rank in a Grand Company. Can be shared with up to 3 friends (see Sharing your house).

Free Company house

Needs FC rank 6, at least 4 members, and 30 days of membership for the buyer. Only FC houses have Private Chambers (300,000 gil each).

Apartment

One per character, 500,000 gil, and it sits outside the house limit. Not subject to demolition. No yard or garden.

Cheapest
FC Private Chambers

300,000 gil for your own room inside your FC's house. Same space and limits as an apartment. Needs level 50 and Second Lieutenant rank. Not refunded if the house is demolished.

A character can hold one personal house and, if they lead an FC, one FC house, plus one apartment. Personal and FC plots cannot be converted into each other. The wiki words the one-house rule two different ways, so confirm in game.

Land is sold by lottery on a 9-day cycle (5 days of entry, 4 of results), one entry per period. A small plot costs 3,000,000 to 3,750,000 gil depending on plot grade. Price does not change by district. The full price is held as a deposit while you wait. Losing entries are refunded in full, and a winner who does not finalize the purchase forfeits 50% of the deposit.

Not the same thing: hiring a vendor for your house also uses "permits". The cheapest is the 3,000 gil Steward Permit for an Estate Manservant or Maidservant.

04

Sharing with friends

The owner of a personal house can make up to 3 friends "tenants". Tenants can teleport to the house and, if you allow it, decorate and garden. The game calls this Estate Sharing. There is no cost.

How to add a tenant

SocialHousingEstate SettingsShared Estate Settings
  1. Add them to your Friend List first. They must be from your home world.
  2. Open the Housing menu (in the Social menu), then go to Estate Settings, pick your estate, and choose Shared Estate Settings. That page can take a minute to load, so wait for it.
  3. Pick your friend from the list and add them as a tenant.
  4. Set their Tenant Authorizations (below).

"Not on your Friend List" error, even though they are? Remove them from your Friend List, add them again, and retry. You also get an error if your friend is already a tenant of two other houses.

What you can let a tenant do

  • Estate Customization: add, remove, and dye furnishings.
  • Gardening: planting, harvesting, and crop disposal, each set separately.
  • Stable Tending: stable chocobos.
  • Orchestrion: play music.
  • Message Book: manage the Message Book.

Good to know

  • Tenants do not reset the demolition timer. Only the owner's visits count, so the owner still has to go in at least once every 45 days.
  • Tenants can teleport home. They can use the estate's Miniature Aetheryte like the owner, and they can use its crafting facilities.
  • Only the owner can reclaim items if the house is demolished.
  • Limits. A player can be a tenant of at most 2 houses. Apartments cannot be shared.
  • Leaving. A tenant can remove themselves from the tenant list at any time.

Not yet sourced: the exact button names on the Shared Estate Settings page, whether your friend has to accept, and how the owner removes a tenant. Check these in game.

Not yet sourced: whether flowerpots, vendors, and crafting facilities also count toward the indoor furnishing total. No wiki page says either way, so check in game before filling a small house to the limit.

06

Flowerpots

A flowerpot is a furnishing that grows one plant indoors. It is not a garden patch, and it is the only way to garden in an apartment or Private Chamber.

pots for your FC roompots for your apartmentpots for your cottagepots for your housepots for your mansion
  • One crop per pot. A garden patch has several beds. A flowerpot has one.
  • Same plants as the garden. Flowerpots grow the same plants and crops as patches, with the same steps (plant, fertilize once per hour, tend, harvest). Some seeds can only be grown in a flowerpot.
  • Indoors. The wiki describes flowerpots as indoor furnishings. It describes no outdoor flowerpot.
  • No crossbreeding. The wiki says intercrossing is not possible in a flowerpot.
  • Pots in storage do not grow. Take them out of the Storeroom to keep a crop going.

The Flowerpot page has no Apartment row, while the Apartments page says "up to two", so the apartment figure is from that page only.

Vendor NPCs

Housing vendors are hired with a permit bought from an Independent Sutler in each residential district, or an Apartment Merchant in apartment lobbies. They share one limit across all types: 4 in an apartment, 6 in a small house, 8 in a medium, 10 in a large. Pick the ones you will really use. A Mender and a Steward are the usual first two.

Crafting facilities

A facility gives a 30-minute buff that raises your maximum CP while you craft on the matching class. It only helps at crafter level 90 or below. A small house holds 4 facilities, so choose the classes you are leveling. They are crafted or bought on the Market Board, with no vendor price. Owners and tenants can use them in a personal house, and any member in an FC house. The wiki lists the CP bonus as +10, which we have not tested.

Visit your house at least once every 45 days or it is demolished. You get a warning in game at 30 days and by email at 35 and 42 days. For a shared estate, only the owner's visit counts, not a tenant's. An FC house counts a visit from any FC member.

The timer is live. Demolition was briefly paused in October 2026 after a security issue. The Lodestone notice says it resumed on October 26, 2026 at 1:00 a.m. PDT, even though the wiki still says "paused". The notice does not say whether timers were reset.

If a house is demolished, you can recover 80% of the plot price from the caretaker within 35 days. Permits and exteriors are not refunded.

Furnishing cheaply

Housing Merchants sell the basics for little. Walls, floors, rugs, and bookshelves run about 5,000 gil, chairs and tables about 2,000, and lamps 3,000 to 4,000. Furnishing a small house from merchants alone is plausibly well under 1,000,000 gil (our estimate, not a sourced figure).

Walls, floors, rugs, bookshelves~5,000
Chairs and tables~2,000
Lamps3,000 to 4,000
Preview first. Merchant items can be previewed before you buy. Items from vendors, retainers, Feast, seasonal events, and the Mog Station cannot.
Other sources. Crafting, the Market Board, garden plants, Island Sanctuary, seasonal events, Feast rewards, and Mog Station items.
Habits worth learning early. Rotate while holding an item, use grid snap, place items on counter surfaces, use Storage Mode, and use "Move to Front Door" if you get stuck.

Which furniture is cheapest to craft, and which comes from the Gold Saucer or achievements, is not covered here yet.
